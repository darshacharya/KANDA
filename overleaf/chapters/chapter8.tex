\chapter{Experimental Results and Conclusions}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter shows what KANDA can actually do. We tried it out by giving it voice commands, asking it to find objects, controlling it remotely via Telegram, running demos, and deliberately breaking things to see if it would handle it gracefully. We measured how fast things were, counted how many times it succeeded, and watched for any weird behaviour. This is where theory meets reality---and where we found both what works really well and what doesn't.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:eval_setup}

\textbf{Hardware.} ESP32 DevKit, Raspberry Pi~4 (4\,GB), Pi Camera v2.1, USB microphone, JBL Go Bluetooth speaker, TB6612FNG dual driver, HC-SR04$\times$3 (with voltage dividers), SSD1306 OLED, differential-drive chassis; USB serial at 115,200 baud. Total bill of materials: under \$70.

\textbf{Software.} Phase~4 firmware; 13-module Python intelligence layer under \texttt{vision\_module/}; Groq Llama~3.3 for text reasoning and ASR; NVIDIA NIM Llama~3.2 Vision for scene understanding; openWakeWord for ``hey jarvis''; espeak-ng (or gTTS) for speech output; picamera2 for the camera.

\textbf{Testing environment.} We ran everything in a 3×4 meter room with some furniture to make it realistic—desks, chairs, a shelf. The room was quiet (less than 40 dB background noise). We had Wi-Fi with low latency to Google Cloud. Most importantly: an emergency power button was always within arm's reach, and one person was always watching the robot.

\textbf{Ethics and safety.} Trials on a clear surface with operator present. Emergency power disconnect within arm's reach. No public-space or outdoor trials.

\section{Voice-Commanded Navigation}
\label{sec:voice_results}

From saying the wake word to the robot starting to move took 4--10 seconds depending on whether it needed to generate a plan or just execute a simple command.

\section{Telegram and Presentation}
\label{sec:telegram_results}

\textbf{Telegram voice notes:} 9/10 transcribed correctly. Single failure caused by heavy background noise (construction).

\textbf{Photo-based search:} Correct object description generated in 10/10 photos. Subsequent ReAct search succeeded at the same 8/10 rate as voice-initiated trials.

\textbf{Presentation mode:} 12-slide talk delivered end-to-end without interruption. Average slide TTS duration: 8--15\,s. Telegram navigation (\texttt{/next}, \texttt{/prev}) responded within 1\,s.

\label{sec:ablation}

Five components were individually removed across 10-trial sessions to quantify their contribution (Table~\ref{tab:ablation_ch8}).

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.8cm}X@{}}

\textbf{Component removed} & \textbf{Observed degradation} \\
\midrule

Obstacle warnings in prompt & +20\% unsafe forward commands when front $<$15\,cm \\

ESP32 firmware obstacle stop & Near-collision when validator-passed ``forward'' at close range \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\textbf{Interpretation.} Each component addresses an orthogonal failure mode. The VLM scene and prompt safety rules provide \emph{specification-level} alignment; the validator provides \emph{runtime} enforcement; firmware stops provide \emph{device-level} last-resort safety. No single layer alone covers all failure classes---consistent with defence-in-depth principles~\cite{amodei2016safety}.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Multimodal grounding.} The body-context assembler with inline VLM descriptions proved essential: Groq avoided forward commands when the scene described a ``wall directly ahead'' even before sensor warnings triggered. The 15\% ablation degradation confirms VLM context as independently necessary.

\textbf{Semantic memory effectiveness.} The 60\% revisitation rate without memory demonstrates its critical role. The auto-lowering threshold prevented permanent loop traps in all tested scenarios.

\textbf{Sensing limitations.} Three radial ultrasonic sensors leave angular blind spots at oblique approach angles. The 2\,cm--400\,cm HC-SR04 range and $\sim$15$^\circ$ beam width mean small or soft objects may be missed.

\textbf{Cloud dependence.} All multimodal reasoning requires HTTPS connectivity. The 1.5--4\,s inference latency constrains maximum safe forward speed in cluttered environments to approximately 0.3\,m/s.

\textbf{Evaluation rigour.} Without motion-capture ground truth, positioning accuracy cannot be quantified. Results are demonstration-grade; formal user studies and instrumented courses remain future work.

\textbf{STT robustness.} Voice recognition degrades in environments above 50\,dB ambient. The wake-word false-positive rate increases near televisions.

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:ch8summary}

\textbf{Conclusions.} KANDA's Phase~4 system demonstrates that multimodal embodied intelligence---voice, vision, language, and locomotion---is achievable on commodity IoT hardware under \$70. The 7-state architecture orchestrates wake-word detection, speech transcription, intent classification, plan execution, and visual search in a predictable, cancellable framework. Empirical results confirm:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Voice command accuracy $>$90\% across 6 command classes (Table~\ref{tab:voice_results})
  \item Visual object search success 80\% with mean 7.3 steps (Table~\ref{tab:search_results})
  \item Safe recovery in all 8 tested fault scenarios (Table~\ref{tab:faults_ch8})
  \item Three-layer safety (prompt + validator + firmware) covers orthogonal failure modes (Table~\ref{tab:ablation_ch8})
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Future work.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Local quantised model (Gemma 2B on Pi) for sub-500\,ms inference without cloud
  \item Depth camera (Intel RealSense or OAK-D Lite) for richer spatial reasoning
  \item On-device Whisper for offline speech-to-text
  \item Encoder-based odometry for position tracking
  \item SLAM integration for named-location navigation
  \item Formal verification of the validator against temporal safety policies~\cite{amodei2016safety}
\end{itemize}
